### Beats per Scene

**Each scene carries 2-4 beats.**

This applies to every structural unit of the storyboard draft, including the hook, key ideas, and payoff. Key ideas consist of 2-4 scenes, with one idea per scene, and each of those scenes carries its own set of 2-4 beats.

### What Is a Beat?

**A beat is an individual narration unit where one beat equals one spoken sentence.**

In other words, narration beats are groups of 2-4 spoken sentences per scene. Beats use words a curious adult knows, with new terms requiring a same-sentence gloss.

Beats are limited to source-supported statements. No beat may state anything the sources do not support, and if a planned beat has no supporting passage, the beat is cut.

## Q2: What must every scene list as a source?

### lite (retrieved chunk)

- Every scene lists >=1 source: the exact quoted passage plus the heading it
  came from (e.g. `Gears > "a 44-tooth ring..."`).

### LightRAG (answer)

**Requirement for Every Scene**

Every scene in a storyboard draft must list **at least one source**. This is a mandatory sourcing rule, and it applies to the hook as one scene, each of the 2-4 key idea scenes, and the payoff as one scene.

**What the Source Must Contain**

A valid source is not a general reference. It must consist of two linked parts together:

* **the exact quoted passage** - verbatim quoted text from the source
* **the heading it came from** - the source section label indicating where the passage came from

For example, the required format is illustrated as `Gears > "a 44-tooth ring..."`, where Gears exemplifies a heading cited together with the exact quoted passage.

Quotes must be **verbatim**. Trimming with [...] is allowed only at the edges, never inside. If a planned beat has no supporting passage, the beat must be cut, not the rule. No beat may state anything the sources do not support.
