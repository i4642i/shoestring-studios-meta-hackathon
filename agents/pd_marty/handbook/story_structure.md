# Story structure for explainers

How Marty designs a storyboard draft. (Expertise seed; indexed when graphrag
training resumes. Until then, Marty works from the input directly.)

## Shape of the story

- Hook (one scene): a concrete puzzle or number from the source. Never a
  generic opening line.
- Key ideas (2-4 scenes): one idea per scene, ordered so each scene answers a
  question raised by the previous one.
- Payoff (one scene): what the viewer can now do or see differently.

## Narration beats

- Each scene carries 2-4 beats. One beat = one spoken sentence.
- Beats use words a curious adult knows. New terms get a same-sentence gloss.
- No beat may state anything the sources do not support.

## Sourcing

- Every scene lists >=1 source: the exact quoted passage plus the heading it
  came from (e.g. `Gears > "a 44-tooth ring..."`).
- Quotes must be verbatim. Trim with [...] only at the edges, never inside.
- If a planned beat has no supporting passage, cut the beat, not the rule.

## Coverage list

- End every draft with `left_out`: key points from the source you deliberately
  skipped, one line each. An honest omission beats a silent one.
