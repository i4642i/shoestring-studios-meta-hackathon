# Intake Examples

Example answers for the 5 sales intake questions. Use these to judge
whether a client's answer is concrete enough — and to suggest options
when the client is vague. Never copy an example into a request as if
the client said it.

## 1. Audience level

- Beginners: "high-school students with no physics background".
- Students: "undergrads who finished one mechanics course".
- Experts: "ML engineers who read papers weekly".
- Public: "curious adults, no math beyond high school".

Vague (ask again): "everyone", "students", "people interested in AI".

## 2. Target video length

- "5 minutes, one idea, no filler."
- "About 10 minutes, standard explainer."
- "20 minutes, lecture-style with chapters."

Vague (ask again): "short", "as long as it takes", "normal length".

## 3. Depth

- Key concepts: "key concepts only — the 3 ideas that matter."
- Comprehensive: "cover every section, nothing skipped."

Vague (ask again): "detailed", "simple", "thorough but easy".

## 4. Theme direction

- "Step-by-step guide, calm and serious."
- "Big-picture overview, playful tone."
- "Real-life examples, story-led."

Vague (ask again): "fun", "professional", "like a documentary".

## 5. Source material

- "The PDF I attached: attention-is-all-you-need.pdf."
- "Chapter 3 of the attached textbook scan."

Missing: no file attached yet — point at the Attach button.

## Full worked example

Client: "I'm a TA. My freshmen fear thermodynamics."
Sam: audience = "college freshmen, beginners, afraid of the topic".
Client: "10 minutes max, they stop watching after that."
Sam: length = "10 minutes".
Client: "Just entropy and the second law, skip the rest."
Sam: depth = "key concepts only (entropy, second law)".
Client: "Something fun, with everyday examples."
Sam: theme = "playful, real-life examples".
Client attaches thermo-ch2.pdf.
Sam: source = "thermo-ch2.pdf". Intake complete.
